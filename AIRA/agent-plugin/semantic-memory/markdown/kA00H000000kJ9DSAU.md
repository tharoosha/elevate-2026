Balancer Understanding User Roles
=================================

Understanding User Roles
------------------------

There are four administrative roles and one user role in Balancer. Administrative roles include **Organization and Engagement Admin**, **Financial Admin** and **Business Administrator**, which are defined in Setup, and **Local Business Admin**, which is configured in Balancer Administration. **Local Business Administrator** roles are assigned to standard users to grant administrative access to specific legal entities.

User roles include Preparers, Approvers, and Reviewers. Business Administrators, Local Business Administrators, and standard users can all be assigned these roles. For example, the Business Administrator may also be a Preparer or an Approver.

While the **Organization and Engagement Admin** and **Financial Admin** is not part of the Balancer hierarchy, many administrators fulfill more than one role. For example, the Financial Admin may also be a Business Admin.

Administrative Roles
--------------------

### System Administrators

The **Organization and Engagement Admin (OEA)** is responsible for system administration. The SA is the initial user of the system and is typically an administrator or part of the IT staff.

*   Manage employees (including SCIM) and Organization Admins
*   Control SSO/password policies
*   Can see all organization engagements; can create engagements (when permitted)
*   Manage user access to a specific engagement
*   Configure engagement settings (time zone, webhooks, integrations)
*   Limited trust actions (add trust to service-provider organizations; remove Support/Auditor)

**Financial admin (FA)** - is responsible for the financial configurations, including: 

*   Create and manage Legal entities
*   Create and manage fiscal years/periods
*   Import and maintain currency rates

The OEA is the first user of the system and is responsible for setting it up by adding users and assigning them access and administrative privileges for applications. However, the OEA cannot activate the roles that are created. Role activation happens only when users complete the registration process by confirming their verification through email.

The FA is responsible for creating the required legal entities. Accounts and user roles can only be created and assigned after these legal entities are in place.

### Balancer Administrators

**Business Administrators (BA)** – The BA is typically a financial manager and is responsible for Balancer administration. Business admin permissions include:

*   Accessing all legal entities.
*   Granting users legal entity access.
*   Importing balances and items.
*   Utilizing task automation jobs.
*   Configuring account settings and roles.
*   Setting up fiscal years and periods.
*   Adding and updating closing rates.
*   Configuring general settings to meet specific business requirements.
*   Setting up import item and balance automation to streamline and simplify reconciliation processes.

**Local Business Administrator (LBA)** – LBA access is given to standard users. The LBA’s admin rights are limited to specific legal entities, as assigned by the BA.  
To grant LBA access, navigate to **Balancer>>Administration>>Users**, click the user’s name, and check the box(es) next to the legal entities that the LBA needs to access. _Refer to Lesson 2 Legal Entity Access_.  
Local Business Admin permissions include:

*   Manually importing balances and items for selected legal entities.
*   Configuring account settings and roles for selected legal entities.
*   Viewing dashboards for selected legal entities.

Non-Administrative User Roles
-----------------------------

All users, including standard users and administrators, can be assigned as Preparers, Approvers, and/or Reviewers for specific accounts. Each user has a personalized Home page that displays tasks assigned to the user.  
**Preparer** – The accounting staff that checks and confirms the reconciliation.  
**Approver** – Typically, a financial or accounting manager who certifies the account reconciliation.  
**Reviewer** – An Auditor who reviews the reconciliation. Reviewers are often finance managers, controllers, risk/compliance managers, external auditors, and chartered accountants.

### Auditors

An Auditor is a user who is responsible for evaluating the validity and reliability of a company or organization's financial statements. Users with Auditor access in **Setup** are limited to viewing and exporting account reconciliations only. The Auditor's view can be limited further by enabling the setting **Show only finalized accounts to auditors** in **General settings**. Auditor users must be assigned **Read access** to specific legal entities in **Administration>>Users.**
